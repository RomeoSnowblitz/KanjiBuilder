For the test Let's take a look at both EN-IT.txt and IT-EN.txt

We will need to make 4 categories (Matches, Exceptions, onlyLangA, onlyLangB)

To search for matches let's take a look at two types of examples

A direct match will look like this:
 
 goodbye - arrivederci
 arrivederci - goodbye

Both of these definitions are defined perfectly as each other
take a look at another Direct example that is more complex

 coffee - caffè
 caffè - coffee

Note that in IT-EN there is a second 
 caffè - coffee tree, cups of coffee, cafes, cup of coffee, coffee shop, café's, café, coffee, espresso, coffee bar, coffees, bar's

However in EN-IT
caffè is only defined as  "caffè - coffee" therefore we can ignore the second line

The next section are Exceptions

An Exception will look like this:

 salve - hello

since there is a Direct Match for
 hello - ciao
 ciao - hello

if there was no direct match then "salve - hello" would be considered an "Italian Only", but since it's defined as "hello" but in English both "hello" is "ciao" and there is no "hello - salve" in IT-EN then it's an Exception

The Last two Categories are for when there is a definition that is not at all found in the other dictionary
for example:

 dedication - consacrazione, dedicazione, dedizione, impegno, dedica

This is only an English Word since there is No match for "dedication" in EN-IT.txt
and the same can be said the other way
